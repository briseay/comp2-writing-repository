Composition II

APA Style

Format the paper. Credit the source. Help your reader find the research.

APA Is a Documentation System

APA style helps readers identify where information came from, understand when it was published, and locate the complete source. Citations and References are not decorations added after the writing is finished. They are part of responsible research.

Setting Up the Document

Formatting Your APA Paper

APA formatting creates a consistent academic document. For papers in this course, use the following setup unless your assignment directions state otherwise.

Font

Use 12-point Times New Roman throughout the paper.

Spacing

Double-space the entire document.

Paragraph Spacing

Set spacing before and after paragraphs to 0 and do not add extra blank space between paragraphs.

Margins

Use one-inch margins on all sides.

Paragraph Indents

Indent the first line of each body paragraph one-half inch.

Page Numbers

Include the page number in the upper-right corner.

Paper Title

Center the title of your paper and place it in bold. Begin the body of the paper on the next line with normal left alignment and standard paragraph indentation.

Microsoft Word Setup

Your APA desktop tutorial walks through setting Times New Roman 12-point font, double spacing, zero paragraph spacing, one-inch margins, page numbering, paper title formatting, and References-page setup.

Quick Word Setup Sequence
  1. Set the font to Times New Roman, 12-point.
  2. Set paragraph spacing before and after to 0.
  3. Set line spacing to double.
  4. Select “Don't add space between paragraphs of the same style.”
  5. Use Normal one-inch margins.
  6. Add the page number in the upper-right corner.
  7. Center and bold the title of the paper.
  8. Return to left alignment before beginning the body.
  9. Indent the first line of each paragraph.

Crediting Sources in the Paper

APA In-Text Citations

APA uses an author-date system. When you use information from a source, readers should be able to identify who created the information and when it was published.

One Author

Narrative: Smith (2024) Parenthetical: (Smith, 2024)

Two Authors

Narrative: Smith and Seay (2024) Parenthetical: (Smith & Seay, 2024)

Three or More Authors

Narrative: Smith et al. (2024) Parenthetical: (Smith et al., 2024)

Direct Quotations

Direct quotations also include a location so readers can find the exact words inside the source.

Author Named in the Sentence

According to Smith (2024), “quoted material appears here” (p. 6).

Author Not Named in the Sentence

The research demonstrates that “quoted material appears here” (Smith, 2024, p. 6).

No Page Number?

When a source does not provide page numbers, use a paragraph number when one can be identified.

Narrative

According to Smith (2024), “quoted material appears here” (para. 6).

Parenthetical

The source states that “quoted material appears here” (Smith, 2024, para. 6).

Narrative vs. Parenthetical

A narrative citation includes the author's name as part of your sentence.

A parenthetical citation places the author and date together in parentheses.

Responsible Source Use

What Needs a Citation?

Citation is not limited to direct quotations. Whenever information or ideas came from another source, the source must receive credit.

Direct Quotations

If you copy the author's exact words, use quotation marks and a citation.

Paraphrases

If you restate source information in your own wording, cite the source.

Summaries

If you condense a source's ideas or findings, cite the source.

Borrowed Ideas

If the idea originated somewhere other than your own thinking, give credit to the source.

Paraphrasing Still Requires Citation

Changing the wording does not make the information yours. Paraphrases must be cited.

A true paraphrase also changes the sentence structure and wording substantially. Replacing a few words with synonyms is not enough.

Accuracy Matters Too

Responsible citation also means representing the source accurately. A citation should point to the correct author, publication, and location. Quoted wording should match the original source and should not be altered in a way that changes its meaning.

Not Responsible Source Use

Change the wording slightly, cite the wrong page, or alter a quote so that it supports the point you wanted to make.

Responsible Source Use

Represent the source accurately, cite the correct information, and build your own analysis from what the research actually says.

Documenting Complete Source Information

Building a References Page

The References page provides the complete publication information for sources cited in the paper. It allows readers to locate those sources and strengthens your credibility as a researcher.

Only Include Sources You Used

If a source is not cited in the paper, do not include it on the References page. Every References entry should connect to an in-text citation.

Formatting the References Page

  • Begin the References page on a new page.
  • Center and bold the heading References.
  • List entries alphabetically by the first author's last name.
  • Do not rearrange the authors listed within a source.
  • Double-space the entire page.
  • Do not add extra spacing between entries.
  • Use a hanging indent for each entry.

Use a Page Break

Insert a page break before the References page instead of pressing Enter repeatedly. This prevents the References page from shifting when you revise the body of the paper.

Academic Journal Article Pattern

Author
Year
Article Title
Journal Title
Volume / Issue / Pages
DOI

Davis, D. S. (2018). Advance directives and Alzheimer's disease. Journal of Law, Medicine, & Ethics, 46(3), 744-748. https://doi.org/10.1177/1073110518804235

Breaking Down the Entry

Author Davis, D. S.
Year (2018).
Article Title Advance directives and Alzheimer's disease.
Journal Title Journal of Law, Medicine, & Ethics,
Source Information 46(3), 744-748.
DOI https://doi.org/10.1177/1073110518804235

Sentence Case

APA uses sentence case for article titles, book titles, and many other source titles. Capitalize the first word of the title, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns.

Not APA Sentence Case

Advance Directives and Alzheimer's Disease

APA Sentence Case

Advance directives and Alzheimer's disease

Formatting Authors

Situation Pattern Example
One Author Last name, initials. Smith, J. R.
Two Authors Separate authors with a comma and use an ampersand before the final author. Smith, J. R., & Seay, B. M.
Multiple Authors Keep authors in the order shown on the source. Smith, J. R., Seay, B. M., & Brown, A. L.

Do Not Reorder Authors

The authors within one source remain in the order provided by the publication. Alphabetical order applies to the list of References entries, not to the authors inside an individual entry.

Common Sources in Composition II

Common APA Reference Patterns

You do not need to memorize every APA source type. Identify what kind of source you have, locate the necessary publication information, and build the entry using the appropriate pattern.

Peer-Reviewed Journal Article

Lastname, A. A. (Year). Title of article. Title of Periodical, volume(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xx.xxx/yyyy

When a DOI is available, use it. If no DOI exists, use an appropriate stable URL when available.

Webpage

Lastname, F. M. (Year, Month Day). Title of page. Site Name. URL

Use this pattern for a webpage, not for an online newspaper or magazine article.

Online Newspaper or Magazine Article

Lastname, F. M. (Year, Month Day). Title of article. Title of Publication. URL

Book

Author, A. A. (Year). Title of work: Subtitle. Publisher.

Stoneman, R. (2008). Alexander the Great: A life in legend. Yale University Press.

Reference or Encyclopedia Entry: Individual Author

Lastname, F. M. (Year). Title of entry. In F. M. Lastname (Ed.), Title of reference work (edition). Publisher. URL or DOI

Reference Entry: No Individual Author

Title of entry. (Year). In F. M. Lastname (Ed.), Title of reference work (edition). Publisher. URL or DOI

Reference Entry: Group Author

Institution or Organization Name. (Year). Title of entry. In Title of reference work. URL

Troubleshooting

Common APA Mistakes

Forgetting the Year

APA in-text citations normally include both the author and year.

Using & Everywhere

Use and between two authors in a narrative citation and & inside a parenthetical citation.

Using Page Numbers for Every Source

Page or paragraph numbers are required for direct quotations, but not every paraphrase needs a locator.

Not Citing Paraphrases

Changing the wording does not remove the need for citation.

Not Citing Summaries

A summary still represents someone else's information or ideas.

Using Title Case for Article Titles

APA Reference entries use sentence case for article titles.

Missing the Hanging Indent

Each References entry uses a hanging indent.

Extra Spaces Between Entries

Double-space the References page without adding blank lines between entries.

Alphabetizing the Authors Inside a Source

Keep authors in the order shown by the publication.

Mismatched Citations and References

Every cited source should have a matching References entry.

Including Unused Sources

Do not add sources to References simply because you read them.

Changing a Quote's Meaning

Never remove or alter wording in a way that misrepresents the original source.

Before You Submit

Final APA Checklist

Use this checklist after revising and editing your paper.

Paper Formatting

  • My paper uses 12-point Times New Roman.
  • My entire document is double-spaced.
  • My paper has one-inch margins.
  • I removed extra spacing between paragraphs.
  • Body paragraphs use first-line indentation.
  • Page numbers appear in the upper-right corner.
  • My paper title is centered and bold.

In-Text Citations

  • I cited all quotations.
  • I cited all paraphrases.
  • I cited all summaries.
  • I cited borrowed ideas.
  • My citations include the correct author and year.
  • Direct quotations include a page or paragraph number when available.
  • I use “and” correctly in narrative citations.
  • I use “&” correctly in parenthetical citations.

Responsible Source Use

  • My paraphrases use genuinely new wording and sentence structure.
  • I represented each source accurately.
  • I did not alter quoted material in a way that changes meaning.
  • I checked citation details for accuracy.
  • My source use supports my own analysis rather than replacing it.

References

  • The References page begins on a new page.
  • The heading References is centered and bold.
  • Entries are alphabetized by the first author's last name.
  • I kept authors within each source in their original order.
  • The page is double-spaced.
  • I removed extra spaces between entries.
  • Every entry uses a hanging indent.
  • Article titles use sentence case.
  • I included a DOI or appropriate URL when required.
  • Every References entry matches a source cited in the paper.

Final Reminder

Always follow the directions for your current assignment. Your course or assignment instructions may include specific requirements beyond these general APA guidelines.
